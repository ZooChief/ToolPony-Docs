# Profit margin and markup guide



Compare gross margin with markup, set a minimum selling price, or work back to the maximum purchase cost. Check the cent just past the target.





## Example



### Input



```
Cost 60, price 100
```



### Expected result



```
40% margin; 66.67% markup
```





<a id="case-study"></a>



## Quote a price at a 34% margin



1. At a cost of 10.00 the mathematical price is 10 ÷ 0.66, about 15.1515. At 15.15 the margin falls below 34%. The lowest cent price that reaches the target is 15.16. Check that selling price to see profit of 5.16 and a margin of about 34.04%.





## Quote a 12% markup to the cent



A unit costs 17.20. A 12% markup gives 19.264 before rounding. At 19.26 the actual markup is about 11.98%. Quoting 19.27 reaches about 12.03%. The same 12% target on revenue calls for a price of at least 19.55.



[Run example](<https://toolpony.com/en/tools/margin-calculator/#example-markup-quote>)







## Set the maximum purchase cost



At a selling price of 20.01 and a 30% gross margin target, the exact cost limit is 14.007. Paying 14.00 still meets the target. Paying 14.01 takes the margin below 30%.

## Calculation method



Gross profit = selling price − cost. Gross margin divides that profit by selling price. Markup divides it by cost. At 0.01 precision, round a minimum price upward and a maximum cost downward.







## Calculation mode

- Selling price

### Price from markup

- Target margin · %
